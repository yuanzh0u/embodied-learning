Abstract

We present ActiveUMI, a framework for a data collection system that transfers in-the-wild human demonstrations to robots capable of complex bimanual manipulation. ActiveUMI couples a portable VR teleoperation kit with sensorized controllers that mirror the robot's end-effectors, bridging human-robot kinematics via precise pose alignment. To ensure mobility and data quality, we introduce several key techniques, including immersive 3D model rendering, a self-contained wearable computer, and efficient calibration methods. ActiveUMI's defining feature is its capture of active, egocentric perception. By recording an operator's deliberate head movements via a head-mounted display, our system learns the crucial link between visual attention and manipulation. We evaluate ActiveUMI on six challenging bimanual tasks. Policies trained exclusively on ActiveUMI data achieve an average success rate of 70% on in-distribution tasks and demonstrate strong generalization, retaining a 56% success rate when tested on novel objects and in new environments. Our results demonstrate that portable data collection systems, when coupled with learned active perception, provide an effective and scalable pathway toward creating generalizable and highly capable real-world robot policies.

AI Overview

Introduction

Robot learning faces a critical challenge: the scarcity of high-quality training data. While large language models thrive on web-scale datasets, robots require diverse manipulation data that is expensive and time-consuming to collect. Existing approaches like in-lab teleoperation, human videos, and simulation each have significant limitations - from high costs and scalability issues to embodiment gaps and sim-to-real transfer problems.

ActiveUMI addresses this challenge by introducing a portable data collection system that captures not just human actions, but also the crucial element of active perception - how humans dynamically move their heads to gather visual information during manipulation tasks. This represents a significant advance over previous Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI) systems, which relied primarily on static wrist-mounted cameras.

ActiveUMI enables portable, "in-the-wild" data collection with active perception capabilities, capturing both human demonstrations and head movements across diverse environments and challenging manipulation tasks.

Technical Architecture

ActiveUMI's hardware architecture combines commercial VR technology with custom modifications to create a portable, high-fidelity data collection system. The core components include:

VR-Based Control System: Modified Meta Quest 3s controllers are rigidly mounted to robot grippers, providing synchronous 6-DoF pose tracking through the headset's inside-out tracking system. Each controller incorporates a micro-motor for intuitive gripper control and fisheye cameras for wrist-eye views.

Active Perception Module: The Meta Quest 3s headset serves dual purposes - acting as a high-precision localization hub through its robust SLAM system, and functioning as a dynamic "head camera" that captures the operator's egocentric viewpoint. This enables the system to record the correlation between visual attention (head movements) and corresponding hand actions.

Portable Design: The entire computational system is contained in a wearable backpack, including a small PC and battery pack, enabling true "in-the-wild" operation without tethering to stationary workstations.

Methodology and Data Collection

The system employs several sophisticated calibration techniques to ensure precise data collection:

  • In-situ environment setup with VR-rendered axes for intuitive workspace alignment
  • Gripper placeholder jigs for consistent starting positions
  • Haptic feedback for zero-point positioning through controller vibrations

During data collection, all sensor and robot data are captured at 30Hz. The system records:

  • 6-DoF poses of both hands (18 DoF total for bimanual manipulation)
  • Gripper states (2 DoF)
  • Head pose (6 DoF) for active perception
  • Multi-camera visual streams (wrist-mounted fisheye cameras plus head camera)

For policy learning, the system uses π₀, a state-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model, fine-tuned for 50k iterations on the collected data.

Experimental Results and Performance

ActiveUMI demonstrates significant improvements over existing approaches across multiple evaluation metrics:

Task Performance: Policies trained on ActiveUMI data achieved a 70% average success rate across six challenging bimanual tasks, including precision manipulation, deformable object handling, and long-horizon sequences. This represents substantial improvements over baselines:

  • UMI with fixed head camera: 42% success rate (44% lower)
  • Wrist-camera-only baseline: 26% success rate (63% lower)

Generalization Capability: When tested on novel objects and environments, ActiveUMI maintained a 56% success rate, while fixed-camera and wrist-only baselines dropped to 16% and 6% respectively. This demonstrates the critical importance of active perception for robust generalization.

Data Collection Efficiency: ActiveUMI significantly outperforms traditional teleoperation in both speed and accuracy:

  • Throughput: 1.49x-2.06x slower than direct human demonstration, compared to 2.63x-3.27x for teleoperation
  • Accuracy: Average Relative Pose Error of 4.0mm, 2.5× more accurate than the original UMI system (10.1mm)

Mixed Training Strategy

An important finding reveals optimal strategies for combining ActiveUMI data with traditional teleoperation:

  • Pure ActiveUMI data: 80% success on shirt folding task
  • Mixed with 10% teleoperated data: 90% success
  • Optimal mix with just 1% teleoperated data: 95% success

This suggests ActiveUMI data is highly sample-efficient and can be effectively fine-tuned with minimal real-world demonstrations.

Significance and Impact

ActiveUMI makes several key contributions to the field of robot learning:

Bridging the Perception Gap: By explicitly capturing and learning from human visual attention patterns, robots can develop more sophisticated visual strategies for handling occlusions, tracking objects, and gathering task-critical information.

Enabling Scalable Data Collection: The portable, low-cost design using consumer VR hardware provides a practical pathway to collect diverse, high-quality datasets necessary for training robot foundation models.

Improving Real-World Performance: The demonstrated success on complex bimanual tasks involving deformable objects, long-horizon planning, and precision manipulation shows the potential for more capable real-world robotic systems.

The research represents an important step toward developing more generalizable and robust robot policies by incorporating human-like active perception strategies into the learning process. This could accelerate the development of robot foundation models and enable more sophisticated robotic applications across industrial, service, and domestic domains.

Universal manipulation interface: In-the-wild robot teaching without in-the-wild robots
This work is cited as the "most related work" to ActiveUMI. It introduced the concept of a simple, portable handheld controller for scalable bimanual data collection, which ActiveUMI directly builds upon and extends by incorporating active perception.
Cheng Chi, Zhenjia Xu, Chuer Pan, Eric Cousineau, Benjamin Burchfiel, Siyuan Feng, Russ Tedrake, and Shuran Song. Universal manipulation interface: In-the-wild robot teaching without in-the-wild robots. arXiv preprint arXiv:2402.10329, 2024.
Vision in action: Learning active perception from human demonstrations
This paper is highlighted as a "closely related work" that also focuses on designing a teleoperation system for active perception. It establishes the importance of learning viewpoint control from human demonstrations, which is the central contribution of the ActiveUMI framework.
Haoyu Xiong, Xiaomeng Xu, Jimmy Wu, Yifan Hou, Jeannette Bohg, and Shuran Song. Vision in action: Learning active perception from human demonstrations. arXiv preprint arXiv:2506.15666, 2025.
π⁰: A vision-language-action flow model for general robot control
This citation is crucial as it provides the specific vision-language-action (VLA) model used for policy learning in the main paper's experiments. The effectiveness of the data collected by ActiveUMI is validated by training and evaluating this particular model.
Kevin Black, Noah Brown, Danny Driess, et al. π⁰: A vision-language-action flow model for general robot control. arXiv preprint arXiv:2410.24164, 2024.
Diffusion policy: Visuomotor policy learning via action diffusion
This paper is a foundational work in modern visuomotor policy learning, cited as part of the context for robot foundation models. It represents the class of powerful learning algorithms that data collection systems like ActiveUMI aim to supply with large-scale, high-quality data.
Cheng Chi, Siyuan Feng, Yilun Du, Zhenjia Xu, Eric Cousineau, Benjamin Burchfiel, and Shuran Song. Diffusion policy: Visuomotor policy learning via action diffusion. RSS, 2023.
