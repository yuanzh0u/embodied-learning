Abstract

Open-vocabulary scene understanding is crucial for robotic applications, enabling robots to comprehend complex 3D environmental contexts and supporting various downstream tasks such as navigation and manipulation. However, existing methods require pre-built complete 3D semantic maps to construct scene graphs for scene understanding, which limits their applicability in robotic scenarios where environments are explored incrementally. To address this challenge, we propose OGScene3D, an open-vocabulary scene understanding system that achieves accurate 3D semantic mapping and scene graph construction incrementally. Our system employs a confidence-based Gaussian semantic representation that jointly models semantic predictions and their reliability, enabling robust scene modeling. Building on this representation, we introduce a hierarchical 3D semantic optimization strategy that achieves semantic consistency through local correspondence establishment and global refinement, thereby constructing globally consistent semantic maps. Moreover, we design a long-term global optimization method that leverages temporal memory of historical observations to enhance semantic predictions. By integrating 2D-3D semantic consistency with Gaussian rendering contribution, this method continuously refines the semantic understanding of the entire scene. Furthermore, we develop a progressive graph construction approach that dynamically creates and updates both nodes and semantic relationships, allowing continuous updating of the 3D scene graphs. Extensive experiments on widely used datasets and real-world scenes demonstrate the effectiveness of our OGScene3D on open-vocabulary scene understanding.

AI Overview

The Challenge of Open-Vocabulary Scene Understanding

For robots to effectively navigate and interact with the physical world, they must go beyond mere geometric mapping. A truly autonomous agent needs to understand the semantic properties of objects—what they are and how they relate to one another. Traditionally, this has been achieved through "closed-set" semantic mapping, where a system is trained to recognize a predefined list of categories like "chair," "table," or "floor." However, real-world environments are unpredictable and contain an almost infinite variety of objects that a robot might encounter for the first time.

Figure 1: OGScene3D performs incremental open-vocabulary 3D semantic mapping and scene graph construction, allowing for dynamic updates as the environment is explored.

Recent developments in foundation models, such as large vision-language models (VLMs), have introduced "open-vocabulary" capabilities, allowing systems to interpret concepts they were not explicitly trained on. Despite this progress, current methods often struggle with memory efficiency, semantic consistency across different viewpoints, and the ability to update their understanding in real-time as they move. Many existing approaches treat scene graph construction—the process of identifying relationships between objects—as an offline task performed only after a full map is completed. OGScene3D addresses these issues by introducing an incremental framework that builds a 3D Gaussian semantic map and a progressive scene graph simultaneously.

Confidence-Aware 3D Gaussian Representation

At the heart of the OGScene3D framework is 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). Unlike traditional point clouds or meshes, 3DGS represents a scene as a collection of flexible, semi-transparent "Gaussians." Each Gaussian GiG_iGi​ is typically defined by its position, orientation, scale, and color. OGScene3D extends this representation by adding two critical semantic attributes: a semantic label sis_isi​ and a semantic confidence score confi\text{conf}_iconfi​.

Figure 2: The system architecture includes hierarchical 3D semantic optimization, long-term global optimization via a memory buffer, and progressive graph construction.

By storing discrete labels rather than high-dimensional feature vectors, the system remains memory-efficient. The inclusion of confi\text{conf}_iconfi​ is a key departure from previous methods. It explicitly models the uncertainty that arises from 2D segmentation errors or poor viewing angles. When the system renders a 2D semantic view from the 3D model, it uses a dual-weighted voting mechanism. In this process, each Gaussian's vote for a pixel's label is weighted by both its geometric opacity (how solid it is) and its semantic confidence. This ensures that only the most reliable and certain information determines the final semantic output.

Hierarchical 3D Semantic Optimization

To maintain a consistent map during incremental exploration, OGScene3D employs a hierarchical optimization strategy. This process begins with an open-set segmentation stage using a combination of YOLO-World for object detection and Segment Anything Model (SAM) for refining masks. These individual 2D segments are then integrated into the 3D scene through local and global refinement steps.

Local 3D Optimization

When a new frame is captured, the system must associate new observations with the existing map. It uses K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) searching to establish spatial correspondences. Two specific metrics guide label assignment:

  1. Semantic Ambiguity Ratio r(m)r(m)r(m): This measures how uncertain the classification of a segment is.
  2. Spatial-Semantic Coherence p(m)p(m)p(m): This evaluates how well a new segment aligns with the existing 3D structures.

Based on these metrics, segments are either assigned existing labels, marked as novel objects, or left unlabeled if they are too ambiguous. The confidence confi\text{conf}_iconfi​ of each Gaussian is then dynamically updated: it increases when new observations confirm the current label and decreases when there is a conflict.

Global 3D Refinement

To prevent the accumulation of errors over time, the system periodically performs global refinement. It identifies clusters of Gaussians with low average confidence and attempts to relabel them by looking at more certain neighboring clusters. It also cleans up individual low-confidence Gaussians, often found at object boundaries, to ensure sharp and accurate semantic transitions.

Figure 3: Local 3D optimization establishes correspondences between new observations and existing Gaussians to refine labels and confidence scores.

Long-term Global Optimization and Memory

A common problem in robotic mapping is that certain areas are only seen briefly or from difficult angles, leading to "holes" or errors in the semantic map. OGScene3D introduces a long-term global optimization module to mitigate this.

The system maintains a memory buffer MMM that stores high-quality historical frames. These frames are selected based on how much "new" geometric information they provide and how distinct their viewpoint is from others in the buffer. The system then evaluates the consistency between the 3D map and these historical 2D views.

For each view in the buffer, the system calculates a 2D-3D semantic consistency score P2D-3D(p)P_{\text{2D-3D}}(p)P2D-3D​(p) by comparing the rendered 3D semantics against the original 2D segmentation. Furthermore, it uses a gradient-based method to determine exactly how much each individual Gaussian contributed to the final image, denoted as Pconf(i)P_{\text{conf}}(i)Pconf​(i). By combining these factors, the system can retroactively improve the confidence and labels of Gaussians in sparsely observed areas using the weight of historical evidence.

Progressive 3D Scene Graph Construction

While a semantic map identifies "what" is "where," a scene graph explains the context. OGScene3D builds a graph S={N,E}S = \{N, E\}S={N,E} where nodes NNN are objects (clusters of Gaussians) and edges EEE are their relationships.

Node and Relationship Discovery

Unlike offline methods, OGScene3D initializes and updates this graph progressively. For each new semantic entity discovered:

  • Captioning: The system extracts multi-view images of the object and uses a VLM (GPT-4o) to generate a descriptive natural language caption.
  • Confidence: A CLIP model calculates a node confidence score CiC_iCi​ by measuring the alignment between the object's appearance and its generated caption.
  • Relationships: Spatial adjacency is analyzed, and a Large Language Model (LLM) determines the relationship (e.g., "placed on," "attached to") based on the objects' tags and relative 3D positions.

Figure 4: The prompts used for the VLM and LLM to generate descriptive captions and infer semantic relationships between objects.

The graph is not static; it is updated periodically. The system selectively updates only those nodes with low confidence or newly added entities. This targeted update strategy allows the robot to maintain an increasingly accurate understanding of its surroundings without the prohibitive computational cost of re-processing the entire scene every frame.

Performance and Real-World Application

The effectiveness of OGScene3D was evaluated across several standard datasets, including Replica and ScanNet. In 2D novel-view semantic segmentation on the Replica dataset, the system achieved a Mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) of 71.77%, significantly outperforming contemporary 3DGS baselines like OpenGS-SLAM and GS-Grouping.

Figure 5: Comparative results on the Replica dataset show that OGScene3D produces cleaner semantic segments and more accurate object boundaries than existing methods.

In 3D semantic segmentation tasks, the system demonstrated a high degree of robustness. Even when initial 2D segmentations were noisy, the confidence-based refinement and hierarchical optimization allowed OGScene3D to correct errors that caused baseline methods to fail. For example, it could successfully distinguish between a rug and a floor even when they shared similar colors—a common failure point for feature-based approaches.

Figure 6: Top-down views of 3D semantic maps illustrate the system's ability to maintain global consistency and correctly identify object categories in complex indoor scenes.

The system's efficiency is also notable. It processed complete ScanNet scenes in roughly 10 minutes, compared to the 31 minutes required by HOV-SG and over two hours for ConceptGraphs. This efficiency, combined with the incremental nature of the mapping, makes it suitable for real-time robotic deployment.

The researchers tested OGScene3D on a Unitree Go2 quadruped robot. The robot was able to navigate an indoor environment and generate high-quality RGB maps and open-set semantic maps simultaneously. This real-world test confirmed that the system could operate on hardware typical of modern mobile robots while providing the high-level semantic data needed for complex task planning.

Figure 7: The OGScene3D system deployed on a Unitree Go2 quadruped robot for real-time indoor semantic mapping.

Conclusion

OGScene3D represents a shift toward more dynamic and robust 3D scene understanding. By integrating confidence-aware 3D Gaussian Splatting with hierarchical semantic optimization and a progressive scene graph construction framework, it overcomes the limitations of offline, closed-set mapping. The ability to handle open-vocabulary concepts incrementally while maintaining global consistency provides a powerful tool for autonomous robots operating in the unconstrained environments of the real world. Future work aims to expand the system beyond spatial relationships to include functional associations between objects, further deepening a robot's contextual awareness.
