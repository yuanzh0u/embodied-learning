PoseVLA: Universal Pose Pretraining for Generalizable Vision-Language-Action Policies

Abstract

Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often suffer from feature collapse and low training efficiency because they entangle high-level perception with sparse, embodiment-specific action supervision. Since these models typically rely on VLM backbones optimized for Visual Question Answering (VQA), they excel at semantic identification but often overlook subtle 3D state variations that dictate distinct action patterns. To resolve these misalignments, we propose Pose-VLA, a decoupled paradigm that separates VLA training into a pre-training phase for extracting universal 3D spatial priors in a unified camera-centric space, and a post-training phase for efficient embodiment alignment within robot-specific action space. By introducing discrete pose tokens as a universal representation, Pose-VLA seamlessly integrates spatial grounding from diverse 3D datasets with geometry-level trajectories from robotic demonstrations. Our framework follows a two-stage pre-training pipeline, establishing fundamental spatial grounding via poses followed by motion alignment through trajectory supervision. Extensive evaluations demonstrate that Pose-VLA achieves state-of-the-art results on RoboTwin 2.0 with a 79.5% average success rate and competitive performance on LIBERO at 96.0%. Real-world experiments further showcase robust generalization across diverse objects using only 100 demonstrations per task, validating the efficiency of our pre-training paradigm.

AI Overview

The Challenge of Physical Grounding in Vision-Language-Action Models

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a major step toward general-purpose embodied intelligence. These systems aim to bridge the gap between high-level human instructions and low-level robotic control. Typically, a VLA leverages a Vision-Language Model (VLM) pre-trained on massive internet datasets to understand the world, which is then fine-tuned or coupled with an action expert to execute physical tasks.

Figure 1: Comparison between existing VLA paradigms and the Pose-VLA approach. While traditional models often entangle perception and action, Pose-VLA uses pose estimation as a proxy task to instill universal spatial priors before aligning with specific robot embodiments.

However, a fundamental discrepancy exists between the objectives of standard VLM pre-training and the requirements of robotic manipulation. Most VLMs are optimized for tasks like Visual Question Answering (VQA) or image-captioning, which focus on categorical recognition and high-level semantics. Robotic control, by contrast, demands a fine-grained understanding of 3D geometry, such as the exact orientation of a handle or the precise distance between a gripper and a target. This "granularity mismatch" often leads to "feature collapse," where the model's rich semantic features fail to capture the subtle spatial variations necessary for robust physical interaction.

Pose-VLA addresses these limitations by introducing a decoupled learning paradigm. Instead of training a model to map images directly to actions, it first pre-trains the model on a universal 3D spatial task: predicting the poses of objects and robot components in a shared, camera-centric coordinate system.

Pose as a Universal Interface

The core innovation of Pose-VLA is the use of 3D pose as a "universal interface" that connects disparate data sources. Internet-scale datasets contain rich visual information but lack robotic actions, while robotic datasets are often too small and specific to a single robot type. By representing both object states and robotic trajectories as sequences of 3D poses, the researchers can train a model on a combined corpus of general 3D detection data and specialized robotic demonstrations.

To make this data readable by a transformer-based VLM, the authors discretize continuous 3D information into specialized "pose tokens." The output of the model is structured as a sequence S=(τ1,τ2,…,τT)S = (\tau_1, \tau_2, \dots, \tau_T)S=(τ1​,τ2​,…,τT​), where each element τt={ct,bt,pt}\tau_t = \{c_t, b_t, p_t\}τt​={ct​,bt​,pt​} is a tuple containing:

  • ctc_tct​: The object category or component name.
  • bt∈R2b_t \in \mathbb{R}^2bt​∈R2: The 2D bounding box center in image coordinates.
  • pt∈SE(3)p_t \in SE(3)pt​∈SE(3): The 3D pose (translation and rotation) relative to the camera.

This representation treats a robot's movement not as a series of abstract motor commands, but as a sequence of goal poses for the end-effector (the "hand" of the robot) within the camera's view. This alignment ensures that the model learns a consistent geometric language that applies whether it is looking at a static bowl or a moving robotic arm.

Architecture: Integrating Spatial Priors

Pose-VLA is built upon the PaliGemma architecture, which uses a SigLip visual encoder. To enhance the model's ability to reason about the 3D world, the researchers move beyond 2D RGB images by incorporating two additional spatial priors: Camera Ray Encoding and Depth Priors.

Figure 2: The Pose-VLA architecture integrates RGB, depth, and raymaps. The model undergoes pre-training for universal spatial knowledge followed by a post-training phase for embodiment alignment.

Camera Ray Encoding

To understand 3D space from a 2D image, the model needs to know the "perspective" of the camera. The authors use the camera's intrinsic matrix KKK to calculate a ray direction K−1[u,v,1]TK^{-1}[u, v, 1]^TK−1[u,v,1]T for every pixel. These "raymaps" are processed and fused with the visual features, providing the model with absolute geometric anchors that describe how the 3D world is projected onto the 2D sensor.

Depth Integration

While RGB images provide color and texture, depth maps provide metric distance. The model takes depth maps DDD and their associated sparsity masks MMM as input. By stacking them as [D,M][D, M][D,M] and embedding them into the latent space, the model gains direct access to the physical scale of the scene. To ensure the model remains robust even when depth sensors are noisy or unavailable, the training process uses "modality masking," where depth or raymap information is randomly omitted.

The Two-Stage Training Paradigm

The training of Pose-VLA is split into two distinct phases: pre-training for universal priors and post-training for embodiment alignment.

1. Pre-training for Universal 3D Priors

In this stage, the model is trained on a massive dataset of 1.4 million images with 6.5 million 3D annotations (including datasets like Omni3D and BOP) alongside 1.55 million robotic trajectories. The objective is next-token prediction:

where ϕ(O)\phi(O)ϕ(O) represents the observation (RGB, depth, raymaps) under the masking strategy. This phase forces the VLM to become a "geometric reasoner," capable of identifying objects and predicting their 3D positions and orientations in a metric space.

2. Post-training for Embodiment Alignment

Once the VLM has a strong grasp of 3D geometry, a lightweight "action expert" is added. This expert is a small neural network that maps the high-level features from the VLM to the specific control signals required by a particular robot. Because the VLM already understands the 3D trajectory required to complete a task (like "open the drawer"), the action expert only needs to learn how to translate those spatial goals into the robot's specific joint movements or velocities. This stage requires significantly less data—often as few as 100 demonstrations per task.

Experimental Evaluation and Results

The researchers evaluated Pose-VLA across 3D grounding benchmarks, simulation environments, and real-world robotic tasks.

3.D Grounding Performance

On the Objectron benchmark, which tests the ability to estimate the 3D boxes of daily objects, Pose-VLA achieved an Average Precision (AP15) of 87.3. This outperformed much larger models, including Qwen3-VL (235B parameters) and several closed-source models. This result suggests that specialized spatial pre-training is more effective for 3D understanding than simply scaling up the size of a general-purpose VLM.

Figure 3: Pose-VLA demonstrates superior accuracy in estimating 3D bounding boxes and orientations compared to general-purpose VLMs, which often struggle with precise spatial localization.

Simulation and Real-World Robotics

In the RoboTwin 2.0 simulation, Pose-VLA achieved a success rate of 79.1% in "Hard" randomized scenarios, a 14% improvement over the π0\pi_0π0​ baseline. In real-world experiments using an Xtrainer robot, Pose-VLA maintained high success rates (average 81.25%) across diverse tasks such as stacking bowls, hanging mugs, and folding towels.

Analysis of Feature Representations

To understand why Pose-VLA performs better, the researchers used T-SNE to visualize the model's internal features. In traditional VLAs, features for different tasks often overlap, leading to confusion. In Pose-VLA, the features are well-separated by task and consistent within each task. This indicates that the 3D pre-training prevents "feature collapse," allowing the model to maintain distinct, robust representations for different physical actions.

Figure 6: Visualization of internal features. Pose-VLA shows much clearer task separation than baselines, explaining its superior generalization and consistency.

Significance and Future Directions

Pose-VLA demonstrates that the bottleneck in robotic VLA models is not just the amount of data, but the type of representation used during training. By moving from 2D semantics to a 3D pose-centric paradigm, the researchers have created a more data-efficient and generalizable framework for embodied AI.

While the current work focuses on rigid-body manipulation, the authors note several avenues for future research. These include extending the pose-token representation to handle deformable objects (like cloth or rope) through keypoint estimation, and scaling the system to handle more complex, dexterous manipulation with multi-fingered hands. Ultimately, Pose-VLA provides a blueprint for building "geometrically aware" foundation models that can quickly adapt to the complexities of the physical world.
