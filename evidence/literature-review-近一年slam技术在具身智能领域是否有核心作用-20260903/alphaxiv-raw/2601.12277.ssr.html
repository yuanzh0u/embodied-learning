An Efficient and Multi-Modal Navigation System with One-Step World Model

WS
Wangtian ShenZiyang Meng
JM
Jinming Ma
MZ
Mingliang Zhou
DX
Diyun Xiang

Abstract

Navigation is a fundamental capability for mobile robots. While the current trend is to use learning-based approaches to replace traditional geometry-based methods, existing end-to-end learning-based policies often struggle with 3D spatial reasoning and lack a comprehensive understanding of physical world dynamics. Integrating world models-which predict future observations conditioned on given actions-with iterative optimization planning offers a promising solution due to their capacity for imagination and flexibility. However, current navigation world models, typically built on pure transformer architectures, often rely on multi-step diffusion processes and autoregressive frame-by-frame generation. These mechanisms result in prohibitive computational latency, rendering real-time deployment impossible. To address this bottleneck, we propose a lightweight navigation world model that adopts a one-step generation paradigm and a 3D U-Net backbone equipped with efficient spatial-temporal attention. This design drastically reduces inference latency, enabling high-frequency control while achieving superior predictive performance. We also integrate this model into an optimization-based planning framework utilizing anchor-based initialization to handle multi-modal goal navigation tasks. Extensive closed-loop experiments in both simulation and real-world environments demonstrate our system's superior efficiency and robustness compared to state-of-the-art baselines.

AI Overview

Introduction

Mobile robot navigation has emerged as one of the most fundamental challenges in robotics, requiring systems to perceive their environment, understand spatial relationships, and plan effective paths toward desired goals. Traditional approaches relied on modular systems that separate localization, mapping, and planning components, but recent advances in deep learning have enabled more unified, end-to-end solutions. However, these learning-based policies often operate as "black boxes," lacking explicit spatial reasoning capabilities that are crucial for robust navigation in complex environments.

This paper introduces an efficient navigation system that combines the benefits of world models—which can "imagine" future observations and provide explicit spatial understanding—with the computational efficiency required for real-time robotic deployment. The proposed approach addresses a critical bottleneck in existing world model-based navigation systems: their prohibitive computational latency that prevents practical real-world deployment.

Figure 1: Overview of the multi-modal navigation system. The world model generates future observations for candidate actions, which are then evaluated by a planner using different goal modalities (image, language, or point goals) to determine optimal navigation actions.

Problem Context and Motivation

Current learning-based navigation approaches fall into two main categories, each with significant limitations. End-to-end policies like GNM, ViNT, and OmniVLA directly map observations to actions using large neural networks, demonstrating impressive generalization but lacking interpretable spatial reasoning. These systems struggle in scenarios requiring deep understanding of 3D geometry and physical dynamics.

World model-based approaches, exemplified by systems like NWM (Navigation World Model), address this limitation by learning to predict future observations conditioned on actions. This enables explicit spatial reasoning and "imagination" of potential outcomes. However, existing world models suffer from computational inefficiency due to iterative generation processes, making them impractical for real-time control loops that require high-frequency decision making.

The key insight driving this research is that navigation-specific world models can be dramatically simplified compared to general-purpose generative models, since they only need to predict plausible futures for planning rather than generate diverse, high-fidelity content for human consumption.

Technical Approach

One-Step World Model Architecture

The core innovation lies in adopting a one-step generation paradigm that eliminates the iterative denoising processes common in diffusion-based world models. The system uses a shortcut training objective that enables direct prediction from noisy observations to clean future frames in a single forward pass.

The world model architecture employs a 3D U-Net backbone that generates 11 future frames simultaneously rather than autoregressively. This parallel generation approach avoids error accumulation and reduces latency significantly. The model operates in a compressed latent space using a pretrained VAE, balancing computational efficiency with generation quality.

Key architectural components include:

The model incorporates action information through cross-attention mechanisms, where robot actions (relative waypoints with position and orientation) are embedded via MLPs and integrated into spatial attention blocks.

Efficient Spatiotemporal Processing

To manage computational complexity while maintaining temporal consistency, the architecture decouples spatial and temporal modeling:

  • Spatial processing: CNN and spatial attention modules operate frame-wise
  • Temporal processing: Dedicated temporal attention with window-based attention mechanisms that restrict operations to local m×mm \times mm×m spatial regions

This design significantly reduces computational cost while preserving spatiotemporal coherence across generated frames.

Multi-Modal Planning Framework

The world model integrates with an optimization-based planner using the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) to evaluate action candidates. The system supports three goal modalities:

  1. Image-goal navigation: Uses LPIPS perceptual similarity to compare predicted observations with target images
  2. Language-goal navigation: Employs SigLIP to measure alignment between predictions and natural language instructions
  3. Point-goal navigation: Incorporates monocular depth estimation to navigate toward specific coordinates while avoiding obstacles

The planner uses an anchor-based initialization strategy rather than purely random sampling, providing better coverage of the action space by initializing candidate trajectories using fixed sets of linear and angular velocities.

Experimental Results

Generation Quality and Efficiency

Quantitative evaluation demonstrates substantial improvements over existing world models. The proposed system achieves superior generation quality across multiple metrics while reducing inference time by approximately 45% compared to NWM baseline:

  • Quality metrics: FVD of 17.254 vs NWM's 27.173, with comparable improvements in PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS
  • Inference speed: 0.076 seconds for 11-frame generation vs 0.138 seconds for NWM
  • Parameter efficiency: 175M parameters vs 194M for NWM

Ablation studies confirm the importance of random trajectory sampling during training and pretraining on large-scale datasets for achieving these performance gains.

Navigation Performance

The system demonstrates consistent superiority across all navigation modalities in both simulation and real-world experiments:

Simulation results (success rate/SPL):

  • Image-goal: 72.67%/69.10% vs NWM 43.33%/38.66%
  • Language-goal: 69.33%/60.90% vs NWM 51.33%/45.98%
  • Point-goal: 50.00%/46.24% vs NWM 52.67%/47.31%

Real-world validation confirms strong sim-to-real transfer, with the proposed method achieving 80% success rate for image-goal navigation compared to 48% for NWM and 52% for OmniVLA.

The anchor-based initialization strategy proves crucial, with ablation studies showing significant performance improvements over random initialization across all goal types.

Contributions and Significance

This work makes several key contributions to the field of embodied AI and robotic navigation:

  1. Computational breakthrough: The one-step generation paradigm makes world model-based planning viable for real-time deployment, addressing a fundamental barrier to practical adoption.

  2. Unified multi-modal framework: Supporting image, language, and point goals within a single system significantly enhances robot versatility and human-robot interaction capabilities.

  3. Superior spatial reasoning: By maintaining explicit world understanding while achieving computational efficiency, the system combines the benefits of both end-to-end policies and traditional world models.

  4. Strong generalization: The pretraining and fine-tuning strategy, validated through extensive real-world experiments, demonstrates robust sim-to-real transfer capabilities.

The collaboration between academic researchers at Tsinghua University and industry practitioners at Xiaomi suggests strong potential for real-world deployment in consumer robotics applications. The work provides a foundation for more sophisticated embodied AI systems that can reason about their environment while operating under the computational constraints of practical robotic platforms.

This research represents a significant step toward making world model-based navigation systems practical for deployment in real-world robotic applications, potentially accelerating the adoption of more intelligent and versatile autonomous systems across various domains.

Navigation world models
This paper introduces NWM, a state-of-the-art navigation world model that serves as the primary baseline for comparison in the main paper's experiments. The main paper's contributions, particularly in efficiency and non-autoregressive generation, are directly contrasted with NWM's autoregressive, diffusion-based approach.

One step diffusion via shortcut models
This citation is fundamental as it introduces the "shortcut training objective" that the main paper adopts to create its efficient one-step generation paradigm. This method is the key to overcoming the high computational latency of previous world models, which is a central claim of the paper.
[Frans et al., 2024] Kevin Frans, Danijar Hafner, Sergey Levine, and Pieter Abbeel. One step diffusion via shortcut models. arXiv preprint arXiv:2410.12557, 2024.
Nomad: Goal masked diffusion policies for navigation and exploration
NoMaD is a state-of-the-art end-to-end diffusion policy that the paper compares against in its navigation experiments. This citation is important because it represents the alternative class of methods (direct policies) that the authors argue have limitations in spatial reasoning, which their world-model-based approach aims to improve upon.
[Sridhar et al., 2024] Ajay Sridhar, Dhruv Shah, Catherine Glossop, and Sergey Levine. Nomad: Goal masked diffusion policies for navigation and exploration. In 2024 IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation (ICRA), pages 63–70. IEEE, 2024.
World models
This is the seminal paper that introduced the concept of "world models," which is the core theoretical foundation of the main paper. The authors build upon this fundamental idea of learning a predictive model of the environment to enable planning and enhance spatial reasoning for navigation.
