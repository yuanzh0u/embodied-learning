Abstract

While large language models have become the prevailing approach for agentic reasoning and planning, their success in symbolic domains does not readily translate to the physical world. Spatial intelligence, the ability to perceive 3D structure, reason about object relationships, and act under physical constraints, is an orthogonal capability that proves important for embodied agents. Existing surveys address either agentic architectures or spatial domains in isolation. None provide a unified framework connecting these complementary capabilities. This paper bridges that gap. Through a thorough review of over 2,000 papers, citing 742 works from top-tier venues, we introduce a unified three-axis taxonomy connecting agentic capabilities with spatial tasks across scales. Crucially, we distinguish spatial grounding (metric understanding of geometry and physics) from symbolic grounding (associating images with text), arguing that perception alone does not confer agency. Our analysis reveals three key findings mapped to these axes: (1) hierarchical memory systems (Capability axis) are important for long-horizon spatial tasks. (2) GNN-LLM integration (Task axis) is a promising approach for structured spatial reasoning. (3) World models (Scale axis) are essential for safe deployment across micro-to-macro spatial scales. We conclude by identifying six grand challenges and outlining directions for future research, including the need for unified evaluation frameworks to standardize cross-domain assessment. This taxonomy provides a foundation for unifying fragmented research efforts and enabling the next generation of spatially-aware autonomous systems in robotics, autonomous vehicles, and geospatial intelligence.

AI Overview

Research Context and Motivation

The development of artificial intelligence has reached a critical juncture where symbolic reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs) must be integrated with real-world spatial understanding. This paper addresses a fundamental limitation: while LLMs excel at symbolic reasoning tasks like text generation and planning, they consistently fail when required to operate in physical environments. The authors identify this failure as stemming from a lack of spatial grounding - a metric understanding of geometry, physics, and the consequences of actions in 3D space, which differs fundamentally from the symbolic grounding that merely associates images with text.

Current research efforts in agentic architectures and spatial domains have largely evolved in isolation, creating a fragmented landscape that hinders the development of truly autonomous systems. The authors argue that real-world applications - from robotic assistants to autonomous vehicles - inherently require both sophisticated reasoning capabilities and accurate environmental understanding. This work positions itself as the first comprehensive framework to systematically bridge these complementary but previously disconnected areas.

Methodological Framework

The authors employed a rigorous systematic literature review methodology spanning multiple academic databases including Google Scholar, arXiv, ACM Digital Library, IEEE Xplore, Semantic Scholar, and DBLP. Their approach involved comprehensive keyword searches targeting terms such as "agentic AI," "spatial intelligence," "embodied AI," and "world models," initially yielding over 3,000 papers.

A multi-stage filtering process refined this corpus through temporal filtering (2018-2026, marking the foundation model era), venue prioritization focusing on top-tier conferences and journals, quality assessment including breakthrough low-citation papers, and relevance filtering for spatial-agentic intersections. Snowball sampling techniques followed citation chains to ensure comprehensive coverage. Two independent reviewers validated the selection process, achieving 94% inter-annotator agreement on inclusion criteria, ultimately resulting in analysis of over 2,000 papers with 742 direct citations.

Core Taxonomic Framework

The paper's primary contribution is a unified three-axis taxonomy that systematically categorizes spatial AI research across multiple dimensions:

Spatial Task Dimension: Four fundamental categories encompass the breadth of spatial reasoning:

  • Navigation: Path planning and locomotion through environments
  • Scene Understanding: Interpreting 3D spatial structures and relationships
  • Manipulation: Physical interaction with objects and environment modification
  • Geospatial Analysis: Large-scale environmental reasoning and planning

Agentic Capability Dimension: Three core capabilities that distinguish intelligent agents:

  • Memory: Hierarchical systems including short-term (in-context), long-term (retrieval-augmented), episodic, and crucially, spatial memory that encodes geometric relationships
  • Planning: Multi-step reasoning for goal achievement in spatial contexts
  • Tool Use & Action: Integration of reasoning with physical or computational tool utilization

Spatial Scale Dimension: Three distinct operational scales with different computational and safety requirements:

  • Micro-spatial (<1m): Fine-grained manipulation tasks like object grasping
  • Meso-spatial (1m-100m): Room-scale navigation and interaction
  • Macro-spatial (>100m): City-scale planning and environmental analysis

This taxonomy provides a structured lens for analyzing research across previously disconnected domains and identifying critical gaps in current approaches.

Key Architectural Insights

The comprehensive analysis reveals several critical architectural patterns essential for effective spatial AI agents:

Hierarchical Memory Systems: Unlike generic language models, spatial agents require sophisticated memory architectures that maintain geometric and topological relationships over time. The authors identify four memory types working in concert: short-term contextual memory, long-term retrieval systems, episodic experience storage, and spatial memory that explicitly encodes 3D relationships. This hierarchical approach addresses fundamental limitations like spatial hallucination and temporal drift that plague language-only agents in physical environments.

GNN-LLM Integration: A particularly promising approach involves integrating Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) with Large Language Models to address structured spatial reasoning. GNNs excel at modeling relational structures inherent in spatial data - object relationships in scenes, road network connectivity, or spatial hierarchies. This integration provides LLMs with explicit spatial memory beyond their context window limitations, combining relational inductive bias with semantic reasoning capabilities.

World Models for Predictive Control: The survey emphasizes world models as essential for enabling agents to predict action consequences and plan safely across spatial scales. By learning latent dynamics of environments, these models allow efficient simulation of future states, critical for avoiding dangerous situations in real-world deployment. The progression from DreamerV2 to DreamerV3 and successful sim-to-real transfer in systems like DayDreamer demonstrate their potential for robust control.

Research Landscape Analysis

The quantitative analysis of 742 cited works reveals significant imbalances in current research efforts. Approximately 68% of methods target meso-spatial tasks like room-scale navigation, while micro-spatial manipulation and macro-spatial geospatial reasoning remain significantly underexplored despite their substantial commercial importance. This distribution suggests major opportunities for cross-scale integration and highlights areas requiring increased research attention.

The authors identify four recurring industry design patterns that have emerged from practical deployments: Human-in-the-Loop Spatial Reasoning for safety-critical applications, Weakly Supervised Planetary-Scale Learning leveraging large but noisy datasets, Agent-Assisted Spatial Workflows augmenting human capabilities, and Embodied AI at Scale with safety as a primary constraint. These patterns provide empirical evidence for how theoretical frameworks translate into real-world systems.

Critical Limitations and Grand Challenges

The paper identifies six fundamental "grand challenges" that represent structural barriers rather than incremental improvements:

  1. Unified Spatial Representation: Developing consistent geometric understanding across scales and modalities
  2. Grounded Long-Horizon Planning: Enabling reliable multi-step reasoning in physical environments
  3. Safe Deployment Under Uncertainty: Ensuring robust operation despite incomplete world knowledge
  4. Sim-to-Real Transfer: Bridging the gap between simulated training and real-world performance
  5. Scalable Multi-Agent Coordination: Enabling effective collaboration among multiple spatial agents
  6. Efficient Edge Deployment: Implementing complex spatial reasoning on resource-constrained platforms

The analysis also reveals critical gaps in current evaluation frameworks. Existing benchmarks fail to adequately assess sim-to-real transfer degradation, cross-scale reasoning capabilities, long-horizon performance over hours or days, and safety-critical failure modes. This absence of comprehensive evaluation hinders progress and obscures true system capabilities.

Implications and Future Directions

This work's significance extends beyond academic categorization to practical implications for developing next-generation autonomous systems. The unified framework enables clearer identification of research priorities, architectural decisions, and collaboration opportunities across previously isolated domains. The emphasis on hierarchical memory, GNN-LLM integration, and world models provides concrete directions for technical development.

For real-world applications, addressing these challenges could revolutionize robotics by enabling complex manipulation in unstructured environments, advance autonomous vehicles beyond current limitations in urban scenarios, and enhance urban computing through integrated macro-scale reasoning. The framework's emphasis on safety, uncertainty quantification, and formal verification is particularly crucial for deployment in high-stakes environments where public trust and regulatory approval are essential.

The proposed conceptual SpatialAgentBench framework addresses evaluation limitations by incorporating sim-to-real assessment, long-horizon performance measurement, safety-critical scenario testing, and cross-scale reasoning evaluation. Such comprehensive benchmarking is essential for measuring true agent capabilities and building trustworthy systems ready for real-world deployment.

World models
This is the seminal paper that introduced the concept of "world models," which the main paper identifies as one of its three key findings essential for safe deployment and predictive planning in agents. It provides the foundational concept for an entire section on enabling technologies.
David Ha and Jurgen Schmidhuber. World models. arXiv preprint arXiv:1803.10122, 2018.
Vision-and-language navigation: Interpreting visually-grounded navigation instructions in real environments
This paper introduced the Room-to-Room (R2R) dataset, establishing the Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) task. VLN is a core meso-scale spatial task that the survey uses as a prime example throughout its analysis, making this reference fundamental to the "Spatial Task" axis of the proposed taxonomy.
Peter Anderson, Qi Wu, Damien Teney, Jake Bruce, Mark Johnson, Niko Sunderhauf, Ian Reid, Stephen Gould, and Anton van den Hengel. Vision-and-language navigation: Interpreting visually-grounded navigation instructions in real environments. In Proceedings of the IEEE Conference on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition, pages 3674–3683, 2018c.
React: Synergizing reasoning and acting in language models
This work introduced the ReAct architecture, which interleaves reasoning and action to create more robust and adaptable agents. The survey paper's definition of agentic AI is built around this iterative cycle of perception, reasoning, and action, making ReAct a foundational architectural pattern for the systems being analyzed.
Shunyu Yao, Jeffrey Zhao, Dian Yu, Nan Du, Izhak Shafran, Karthik Narasimhan, and Yuan Cao. React: Synergizing reasoning and acting in language models. arXiv preprint arXiv:2210.03629, 2023b.
Mastering diverse domains through world models
This paper presents DreamerV3, a state-of-the-art world model that demonstrates cross-domain mastery with a single algorithm. It serves as a powerful, contemporary example supporting the survey's key finding that world models are essential for creating generalist agents capable of planning across various spatial scales.
Danijar Hafner, Jurgis Pasukonis, Jimmy Ba, and Timothy Lillicrap. Mastering diverse domains through world models. arXiv preprint arXiv:2301.04104, 2023.
Memgpt: Towards llms as operating systems
This paper is cited as direct evidence for the survey's first key finding: the importance of hierarchical memory systems for long-horizon spatial tasks. It provides a concrete and influential architecture for memory management, a core component of the "Agentic Capability" axis in the paper's taxonomy.
Charles Packer, Sarah Wooders, Kevin Lin, Vivian Fang, Shishir G Patil, Ion Stoica, and Joseph E Gonzalez. Memgpt: Towards llms as operating systems. arXiv preprint arXiv:2310.08560, 2023.
