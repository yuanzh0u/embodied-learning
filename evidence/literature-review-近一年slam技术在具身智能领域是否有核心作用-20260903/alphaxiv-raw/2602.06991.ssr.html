Abstract

In this paper, we propose a RGB-D SLAM system that reconstructs a language-aligned dense feature field while sustaining low-latency tracking and mapping. First, we introduce a Top-K Rendering pipeline, a high-throughput and semantic-distortion-free method for efficiently rendering high-dimensional feature maps. To address the resulting semantic-geometric discrepancy and mitigate the memory consumption, we further design a multi-criteria map management strategy that prunes redundant or inconsistent Gaussians while preserving scene integrity. Finally, a hybrid field optimization framework jointly refines the geometric and semantic fields under real-time constraints by decoupling their optimization frequencies according to field characteristics. The proposed system achieves superior geometric fidelity compared to geometric-only baselines and comparable semantic fidelity to offline approaches while operating at 15 FPS. Our results demonstrate that online SLAM with dense, uncompressed language-aligned feature fields is both feasible and effective, bridging the gap between 3D perception and language-based reasoning.

AI Overview

Introduction to Language-Aligned 3D Perception

The ability for autonomous systems to navigate and understand their environment is a fundamental requirement in robotics. Traditionally, Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) systems have focused on geometric reconstruction—creating a 3D map of a scene while simultaneously keeping track of the camera's location within it. While geometry tells a robot "where things are," it does not necessarily tell the robot "what things are." To bridge this gap, semantic SLAM adds category labels (like "chair" or "table") to the map.

However, traditional semantic SLAM is often limited by closed-set labels, meaning the system can only recognize objects it was specifically trained on. With the rise of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP, there is a shift toward "open-vocabulary" perception. In this paradigm, a robot can find any object described in natural language, such as "the blue coffee mug near the window," even if that specific category wasn't in its original training data.

LangGS-SLAM introduces a method to build these language-aligned 3D maps in real-time. By utilizing 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), a technique that represents scenes as a collection of flexible 3D "splats" or Gaussians, the researchers have embedded high-dimensional language features directly into the spatial map. This allows for direct interaction between 3D spatial data and language-based reasoning engines.

The Challenge of High-Dimensional Feature Rendering

The primary technical hurdle in creating a language-aligned SLAM system is the computational cost of high-dimensional data. Most 3DGS systems handle color (3 dimensions) and geometry quite efficiently. However, VLM features (like those from LSeg or CLIP) can have 512 dimensions or more.

LangGS-SLAM Architecture

The system operates using two parallel threads: Tracking and Mapping. This separation is common in modern SLAM to ensure that the camera's position can be updated rapidly even while the 3D map is being refined.

Figure 3: The overall pipeline of LangGS-SLAM. It takes RGB-D and VLM feature inputs, tracks the camera pose via G-ICP, and builds a language-aligned 3DGS field through hybrid optimization and intelligent map management.

Tracking

Tracking determines the camera's motion between frames. LangGS-SLAM uses Generalized Iterative Closest Point (G-ICP) to align the current depth frame with the existing 3D map. To ensure robustness, the system also performs a rendering-based refinement step, where the current camera pose is adjusted to minimize the difference between the rendered map and the actual observed image.

Mapping and Keyframe Selection

Not every frame is added to the map. The system selects "keyframes" based on the camera's movement or how much of the scene is already covered. When a new keyframe is selected, new Gaussians are initialized. Each Gaussian carries geometric properties (position, scale, rotation, opacity), a color, and a high-dimensional VLM feature vector derived from the input LSeg features.

Multi-Criteria Map Management

Storing a 512-dimensional vector for every single Gaussian in a map containing millions of points would quickly exhaust GPU memory. LangGS-SLAM uses two main strategies to keep the map compact and consistent.

1. Redundancy-aware Insertion

Before adding new Gaussians to the map, the system checks if the area is already well-represented. During the tracking phase, the system calculates the distance between new points and existing Gaussians. If a new point is very close to an existing one, it is considered redundant and discarded. This prevents the map from becoming unnecessarily dense in static areas.

2. Semantic-Geometric Consistency Pruning

Because the system uses a "hybrid" approach—standard alpha-blending for geometry and Top-K for semantics—the two fields can sometimes become inconsistent. For example, a Gaussian might be useful for reconstruction but contain noisy or incorrect semantic information.

The system identifies pruning candidates GpruneG_{prune}Gprune​ by looking for Gaussians that have low contribution weights during the Top-K semantic rendering. To ensure that essential geometric structures aren't deleted, it assigns each candidate a "geometric importance score." It then uses a probabilistic approach to prune the Gaussians: those with low semantic contribution and low geometric importance are the most likely to be removed. This keeps the map focused on high-quality, consistent data.

Semantics (what things are) tend to be smoother across surfaces. LangGS-SLAM updates the geometry parameters more often than the high-dimensional features. This scheduling avoids redundant calculations, stabilizes the training process, and allows the semantic field to be built upon a solid geometric foundation.

Experimental Evaluation

The researchers evaluated LangGS-SLAM on standard benchmarks like the Replica and TUM-RGBD datasets.

Geometric and Tracking Performance

Despite the added complexity of language features, LangGS-SLAM maintains state-of-the-art tracking accuracy. On the Replica dataset, it achieved an average Trajectory Error (ATE RMSE) of 0.213 cm. In terms of image quality (measured by PSNR), it outperformed other 3DGS-based SLAM systems like SplaTAM and MonoGS while running significantly faster (15 FPS vs. roughly 1-2 FPS for the others).

Semantic Performance

The system was compared against offline open-vocabulary reconstruction methods like LeRF and LangSplat. Surprisingly, LangGS-SLAM achieved higher semantic accuracy and mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) than many offline methods. This is likely due to the Top-K rendering and pruning strategies, which effectively filter out the "floaters" and noise that often plague 3D reconstructions.

Significance of the Work

LangGS-SLAM represents a shift in how robots can perceive the world in real-time. By moving away from fixed semantic labels and toward continuous language-aligned embeddings, it enables a more flexible and intelligent interaction with 3D space.

The specific technical contributions—Top-K rendering and multi-criteria map management—solve the "rendering bottleneck" and "memory explosion" that previously made high-dimensional feature fields impractical for online SLAM. The result is a system that can see a room, build a 3D model of it, and understand what every object in that room is through natural language, all while processing data at 15 frames per second on a standard desktop GPU. This provides a robust foundation for future embodied AI systems that need to navigate and reason about complex, real-world environments.
