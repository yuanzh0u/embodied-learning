Abstract

Despite recent progress in calibration-free monocular SLAM via 3D vision foundation models, scale drift remains severe on long sequences. Motion-agnostic partitioning breaks contextual coherence and causes zero-motion drift, while conventional geometric alignment is computationally expensive. To address these issues, we propose VGGT-Motion, a calibration-free SLAM system for efficient and robust global consistency over kilometer-scale trajectories. Specifically, we first propose a motion-aware submap construction mechanism that uses optical flow to guide adaptive partitioning, prune static redundancy, and encapsulate turns for stable local geometry. We then design an anchor-driven direct Sim(3) registration strategy. By exploiting context-balanced anchors, it achieves search-free, pixel-wise dense alignment and efficient loop closure without costly feature matching. Finally, a lightweight submap-level pose graph optimization enforces global consistency with linear complexity, enabling scalable long-range operation. Experiments show that VGGT-Motion markedly improves trajectory accuracy and efficiency, achieving state-of-the-art performance in zero-shot, long-range calibration-free monocular SLAM.

Problem Statement and Context

Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) is a fundamental challenge in robotics and computer vision, where a system must simultaneously determine its position in an environment and construct a map of that environment. While traditional SLAM systems have achieved remarkable success, they typically require known camera calibration parameters and struggle in unconstrained real-world scenarios due to their reliance on handcrafted features and sensitivity to environmental variations.

Recent advances in 3D vision foundation models, such as DUSt3R, MASt3R, and VGGT, have introduced a paradigm shift by enabling joint inference of camera pose, intrinsics, and dense 3D geometry from raw image data. However, these powerful models face a critical limitation: their Transformer-based architectures exhibit quadratic computational complexity with respect to the number of input frames, severely restricting their application to long video sequences or kilometer-scale trajectories common in autonomous driving.

VGGT-Motion addresses this fundamental scalability challenge while maintaining the benefits of calibration-free operation. The system tackles three primary issues that arise when applying foundation models to long-range SLAM: geometric fragmentation from motion-agnostic partitioning, spatio-temporal redundancy leading to zero-motion drift, and contextual asymmetry causing systematic alignment bias.

Motion-Aware Submap Construction

The core innovation of VGGT-Motion lies in its intelligent approach to organizing temporal data based on camera motion dynamics. Rather than using fixed-interval chunking like previous methods, the system implements a sophisticated motion-aware partitioning strategy.

The process begins with motion state estimation using optical flow between consecutive frames. Two key metrics are computed:

mturn(t)=Aggregated lateral flow magnitudem_{turn}^{(t)} = \text{Aggregated lateral flow magnitude}mturn(t)​=Aggregated lateral flow magnitude

These metrics feed into a hierarchical classifier that assigns each frame to one of three motion states: Static (S), Turning (T), or Linear (L). This classification enables intelligent frame selection and submap organization:

Redundancy Filtering removes unnecessary computational load by:

  • Static Redundancy Pruning: During stationary periods, only boundary frames are retained, preventing the foundation model from hallucinating movement due to sensor noise
  • Parallax-Based Keyframe Selection: For dynamic intervals, frames are selected as keyframes only when the parallax relative to the previous keyframe exceeds a threshold τ_palx

Topology-Aware Partitioning preserves geometric coherence by:

  • Turning Segment Encapsulation: Continuous turning intervals are kept within single submaps to maintain the contextual coherence essential for robust scale estimation
  • Adaptive Linear Slicing: Linear motion segments are divided based on a maximum frame budget N_max to balance memory constraints with geometric fidelity

Each submap M_k is composed of the base segment R_k plus overlapping keyframes O_k for seamless alignment and historical loop keyframes L_k when loop closures are detected.

Anchor-Driven Direct Registration

To integrate submaps into a globally consistent trajectory, VGGT-Motion introduces an efficient registration mechanism that addresses the contextual asymmetry problem inherent in Transformer-based models. The key insight is that identical frames can produce different 3D reconstructions when processed within different temporal contexts.

The system employs context-balanced anchor selection:

  • For adjacent submaps, an overlap anchor I_ovlp is chosen at the midpoint of the overlap region to balance the receptive field
  • For loop closure, the historical keyframe with highest similarity serves as the loop anchor I_loop

Because both point maps P_i and P_j are inferred from the same anchor frame I_a_ij, they share pixel-wise alignment:

Global Optimization Framework

The final stage performs lightweight pose graph optimization at the submap level, significantly reducing complexity compared to frame-level bundle adjustment. Submap poses X_k ∈ Sim(3) form graph nodes, while relative Sim(3) constraints from overlap and loop closure form weighted edges.

Experimental Results and Performance

VGGT-Motion demonstrates exceptional performance across multiple challenging datasets. On kilometer-scale sequences from 4Seasons, Complex Urban, and A2D2 datasets, the system achieves an impressive 85-95% reduction in both Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) and translation drift compared to the previous state-of-the-art VGGT-Long. Even on standard benchmarks, it shows notable improvements: 13% better ATE on KITTI and 20% improvement on the dynamic Waymo Open dataset.

The computational efficiency gains are equally remarkable, with 18-36x speedup in total processing time over VGGT-Long on long sequences. This efficiency stems from the redundancy filtering mechanism and the linear complexity of the anchor-driven registration process.

Comprehensive ablation studies validate each component's contribution:

  • Parallax-based keyframe selection significantly outperforms naive sampling strategies
  • Static redundancy pruning eliminates noise-induced drift during stationary periods
  • Turning segment encapsulation maintains structural integrity and prevents scale fractures
  • Anchor-driven registration consistently outperforms baseline dense frame-wise methods

Contributions and Significance

VGGT-Motion makes several key contributions to the field of monocular SLAM:

  1. Motion-Aware Data Organization: The system introduces a principled approach to temporal data structuring based on camera dynamics, demonstrating that global consistency is not solely an optimization problem but also depends on intelligent representation and motion awareness.

  2. Scalable Foundation Model Integration: By addressing the quadratic complexity bottleneck of Transformer-based models, VGGT-Motion enables practical deployment of powerful 3D vision foundation models for real-world, long-range applications.

  3. Calibration-Free Long-Range Operation: The system maintains high accuracy over kilometer-scale trajectories without requiring camera calibration, making it highly suitable for autonomous driving and mobile robotics applications.

  4. Modular Architecture: The framework's model-agnostic design allows integration of various 3D vision foundation models without architectural changes, ensuring adaptability to future advances in the field.

The work represents a significant advancement in making foundation model-based SLAM practical for real-world deployment, particularly in scenarios requiring robust performance over extended trajectories in unconstrained environments. Its combination of accuracy, efficiency, and flexibility positions it as an important step toward more capable autonomous systems.
