Efficient Image-Goal Navigation with Representative Latent World Model

ZZ
Zhiwei Zhang
HZ
Hui ZhangKaihong Huang
CS
Chenghao ShiHuimin Lu

Abstract

World models enable robots to conduct counterfactual reasoning in physical environments by predicting future world states. While conventional approaches often prioritize pixel-level reconstruction of future scenes, such detailed rendering is computationally intensive and unnecessary for planning tasks like navigation. We therefore propose that prediction and planning can be efficiently performed directly within a latent space of high-level semantic representations. To realize this, we introduce the Representative Latent space Navigation World Model (ReL-NWM). Rather than relying on reconstructionoriented latent embeddings, our method leverages a pre-trained representation encoder, DINOv3, and incorporates specialized mechanisms to effectively integrate action signals and historical context within this representation space. By operating entirely in the latent domain, our model bypasses expensive explicit reconstruction and achieves highly efficient navigation planning. Experiments show state-of-the-art trajectory prediction and image-goal navigation performance on multiple benchmarks. Additionally, we demonstrate real-world applicability by deploying the system on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, confirming its efficiency and robustness in practical navigation scenarios.

AI Overview

Understanding Image-Goal Navigation with World Models

Image-goal navigation represents a fundamental challenge in robotics where an agent must reach a goal location specified only by a target image. Traditional approaches have relied on modular pipelines that separate localization, mapping, and planning into distinct components. While robust in controlled settings, these systems suffer from error propagation between modules and lack adaptability to complex, dynamic environments.

Recent advances have shifted toward end-to-end learning frameworks that directly map sensory inputs to actions. Within this paradigm, world models have emerged as particularly promising - they allow robots to build internal simulations of their environment, enabling predictive planning through "mental imagery." However, existing world model approaches face a critical limitation: they prioritize pixel-level reconstruction of future scenes, which is computationally expensive and impractical for real-time robot control.

This paper introduces ReL-NWM (Representative Latent Navigation World Model), which challenges the conventional assumption that pixel-perfect reconstruction is necessary for effective navigation planning. Instead, it operates entirely within a semantically rich latent space, achieving both superior performance and dramatic computational efficiency gains.

Core Methodology and Architecture

ReL-NWM consists of three main components that work together to enable efficient navigation planning without pixel reconstruction.

Representative Latent State Embedding

The foundation of ReL-NWM is its use of a pre-trained DINOv3 Vision Transformer as the observation encoder, replacing the Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) commonly used in previous world models. This choice is crucial - while VAEs are optimized for pixel-level reconstruction, DINOv3 produces high-level semantic representations that capture navigation-critical information like scene geometry and obstacle layouts.

Visual observations are processed into patch-wise token representations, with spatiotemporal positional encodings maintaining structural coherence across historical windows. Robot actions (comprising forward/lateral translation and rotation) are encoded using sinusoidal embeddings processed through multilayer perceptrons.

Transition Model with Advanced Conditioning

The transition model predicts future latent states through several sophisticated mechanisms:

TokenLearner Compression: A TokenLearner module compresses dense spatiotemporal sequences from L tokens to K salient tokens (where K ≪ L), creating a computational bottleneck that forces the model to distill only critical information for long-horizon planning.

FiLM-based Action Integration: Rather than simple concatenation, the model uses Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) for action conditioning. Actions are projected into scale (γ) and shift (β) parameters that multiplicatively and additively modulate feature channels:

FiLM(Sttl,zt)=γt⊙Sttl+βt\text{FiLM}(S^{tl}_t, z_t) = \gamma_t \odot S^{tl}_t + \beta_tFiLM(Sttl​,zt​)=γt​⊙Sttl​+βt​

This mechanism treats actions as dynamic operators, ensuring predicted future states are tightly governed by the agent's intended motion.

SpatioTemporal Cross-Attention: The model uses cross-attention where features from the most recent observation serve as queries, while the entire fused historical sequence provides keys and values. This allows selective attention to relevant historical patterns for next-state prediction.

Training and Navigation Strategy

The world model is trained using multi-step autoregressive supervision with a composite loss function combining Mean Squared Error and Cosine Similarity components:

Ltotal=∑k=1N[α⋅LMSE(s^t+k,st+k)+(1−α)⋅LCosSim(s^t+k,st+k)]L_{total} = \sum_{k=1}^{N} \left[ \alpha \cdot L_{MSE}(\hat{s}_{t+k}, s_{t+k}) + (1-\alpha) \cdot L_{CosSim}(\hat{s}_{t+k}, s_{t+k}) \right]Ltotal​=k=1∑N​[α⋅LMSE​(s^t+k​,st+k​)+(1−α)⋅LCosSim​(s^t+k​,st+k​)]

For navigation, ReL-NWM integrates with Model Predictive Control (MPC). Rather than expensive pixel reconstruction, optimal actions are chosen by minimizing an energy function defined as 1 - CosSim(ŝ_t, s_g), where ŝ_t is the predicted final latent state and s_g is the goal image representation.

Experimental Results and Performance

Trajectory Prediction Accuracy

ReL-NWM achieved state-of-the-art performance on challenging datasets including RECON and HuRoN. On RECON, it attained an Average Trajectory Error (ATE) of 1.09 and Relative Pose Error (RPE) of 0.30, significantly outperforming baselines including previous world model approaches like NWM and LS-NWM.

Ablation studies confirmed the importance of each component:

  • Replacing DINOv2 with VAE increased ATE from 1.09 to 2.19 (100% increase)
  • Replacing FiLM with concatenation increased ATE by 63% to 1.78
  • Substituting cross-attention with CNN increased ATE by 50% to 1.65

Navigation Performance and Computational Efficiency

In Habitat-Sim environments, ReL-NWM achieved 60% Success Rate and 48.5% Success weighted by Path Length, substantially outperforming baselines. Most significantly, it achieved an average inference time of just 1.49 seconds per planning step - representing a 3x speedup over LS-NWM (5.21s) and a remarkable 36x speedup over NWM (55.31s).

Real-World Deployment

The system was successfully deployed on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot with NVIDIA Jetson Orin hardware, achieving 66.7% success rate across 9 trials in challenging corridor navigation tasks. The robot operated at approximately 1 Hz planning frequency, confirming practical real-time capability.

Key Innovations and Contributions

ReL-NWM introduces several important innovations that advance the field of autonomous navigation:

Reconstruction-Free Operation: By eliminating pixel-level reconstruction requirements, the approach dramatically reduces computational overhead while maintaining superior performance. This challenges the prevailing assumption that detailed visual generation is necessary for effective planning.

Foundation Model Integration: The strategic use of DINOv3 demonstrates how pre-trained vision transformers can provide robust, semantically rich representations that generalize better than task-specific encoders optimized for reconstruction.

Advanced Action-State Coupling: The FiLM mechanism provides a principled way to integrate action information into state representations, ensuring that predicted futures are physically consistent and responsive to agent intentions.

Efficient Temporal Modeling: The combination of TokenLearner compression and SpatioTemporal Cross-Attention enables effective long-horizon reasoning while maintaining computational tractability.

Significance and Future Implications

ReL-NWM represents a significant advancement toward practical world model-based navigation for real-world robotics. By achieving dramatic efficiency improvements (36x speedup) while maintaining superior accuracy, it bridges the gap between sophisticated planning capabilities and real-time operational requirements.

The successful deployment on edge computing hardware demonstrates immediate applicability to resource-constrained robotic platforms. This efficiency enables deployment in domains like service robotics, logistics, and exploration where onboard processing is paramount.

The approach also validates the power of foundation models in robotics, showing how pre-trained visual representations can provide more robust and generalizable features than task-specific alternatives. This points toward future developments where large-scale pre-training increasingly benefits robotic applications.

The hierarchical control framework demonstrated with the humanoid robot provides a practical blueprint for integrating model-based planning with reactive control systems, offering a path forward for complex robotic tasks requiring both high-level reasoning and precise execution.
